What to Make With Leftover Baked Potatoes: 4 Dinner Routes That Start With One Cooked Potato

A cooked baked potato already has one useful quality: the slow part is done. From there, dinner can go in four directions. Choose the route that fits the other ingredients you have, the texture you want, and how much energy you have left.

Quick answer

Use leftover baked potatoes as a ready-to-crisp base, a container for fillings, a shortcut for a one-pan skillet, or a crunchy finish for soup and salads. You do not need a full recipe to make the choice. Start with this:

Cooked potatoes vary in size, so treat the quantities below as flexible. Add enough of the extras to make the plate feel complete.

  • Have cheese, beans, or cooked vegetables? Stuff the potatoes.
  • Have an oven, air fryer, or a hot pan? Smash and crisp them.
  • Have eggs, greens, or a little protein? Cube them into a skillet dinner.
  • Have soup or a simple salad? Make crisp potato croutons for texture and substance.

Route 1: Stuffed baked potatoes for the easiest complete meal

This route works when the potatoes are still whole or mostly intact. Split each one open, rough up the center with a fork, and add a filling that can warm through quickly.

A simple filling formula

Choose one item from each column:

Base Something savory Something creamy or melty Fresh finish

Warm the filling in a pan or microwave, pile it into the potatoes, then return them to a hot oven or air fryer until the top is bubbling and the skin is hot again.

Best for: the night when you have several small leftovers that do not look like a meal on their own.

If you also have...

  • Black beans and salsa: make a quick bean-and-cheese stuffed potato.
  • Broccoli and cheddar: chop the broccoli small, warm it with the potato centers, then add cheese.
  • Cooked lentils: season them with garlic, cumin, or a spoonful of tomato sauce and finish with yogurt or herbs.

Route 2: Smash them crisp when you want a sheet-pan dinner

Smashing turns a soft cooked potato into something with plenty of crisp edges. It is especially useful for smaller potatoes or potatoes that have broken a little in the fridge.

For a more substantial tray, roast a quick vegetable alongside the potatoes. Onion wedges, mushrooms, cherry tomatoes, or broccoli work well because they cook in the same window.

Best for: a crisp, low-effort dinner with a dip, a fried egg, or a simple salad.

  1. Heat the oven to a high temperature, around 220°C / 425°F.
  2. Put the potatoes on an oiled tray and press each one down with a glass, mug, or sturdy spatula until it cracks open.
  3. Add oil, salt, pepper, and any herbs or spices you like.
  4. Roast until the edges are crisp and browned.
  5. Add a topping near the end: cheese, sliced onion, chickpeas, chopped peppers, or a fried egg after roasting.

Route 3: Cube the potatoes into a one-pan skillet

A baked potato is already tender, so cubing it gives you a fast start on a skillet dinner. The goal is to brown the cut sides first, then add whichever ingredients need only a few minutes.

The skillet sequence

  1. Cut the potatoes into bite-size cubes.
  2. Brown them in a wide pan with a little oil until several sides are golden.
  3. Add one flavor builder: onion, garlic, spices, pesto, curry paste, or a squeeze of lemon at the end.
  4. Add one quick-cooking extra: greens, frozen peas, corn, mushrooms, or chopped leftover vegetables.
  5. Finish with eggs, cheese, beans, cooked chicken, or a spoonful of yogurt.

Four easy combinations

Best for: one pan, a few loose ingredients, and a dinner that can absorb substitutions.

  • Potato + egg + greens: crack eggs into small gaps in the skillet, cover, and cook until set to your liking.
  • Potato + mushroom + cheese: brown mushrooms first, add potato cubes, then melt cheese over the top.
  • Potato + chickpea + smoky spices: add paprika and cumin, then finish with lemon and herbs.
  • Potato + frozen peas + pesto: stir pesto through at the end, off the heat, so it stays bright.

Route 4: Turn them into crisp potato croutons or a rough topping

If you only have one baked potato left, it can still make another dinner feel more complete. Cut it into small cubes or rough chunks, toss with oil and seasoning, and roast or pan-fry until crisp.

Use the pieces as a topping for:

The potato adds warmth and texture, which is often the missing piece in a light meal. A handful of crisp potato pieces can stretch a soup-and-salad night into dinner.

Best for: the last potato, especially when you are building around something already simmering or roasting.

  • a bowl of tomato, lentil, or vegetable soup;
  • a salad with beans, cheese, or a soft egg;
  • a tray of roasted vegetables;
  • a simple bowl of greens and grains.

A few kitchen moves that keep the choice easy

The best route depends less on having a perfect set of ingredients and more on seeing the baked potato as a head start. Once you choose the texture you want, the rest of dinner becomes much easier to assemble.

  • Keep the skin on when it is in good shape; it adds texture and saves prep.
  • Add watery ingredients late in a skillet so the potatoes can brown first.
  • Use a sauce, cheese, herbs, or a squeeze of citrus at the end to connect the ingredients.
  • Build around what is already cooked before opening something new.
