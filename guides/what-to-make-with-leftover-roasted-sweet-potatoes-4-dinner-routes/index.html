What to Make With Leftover Roasted Sweet Potatoes: 4 Dinner Routes

Roasted sweet potatoes already bring caramelized edges, soft centers, and enough flavour to steer dinner in a useful direction. The only decision is how you want them to behave next: crisp, tucked into something saucy, blended into a bowl, or baked with a few supporting ingredients.

Start with the texture in front of you. Firm cubes can take heat in a skillet or tray. Softer pieces are great for cakes, fillings, and a quick soup finish. The four routes below help you turn that one cooked component into a meal shape that fits the evening.

1. Make crispy skillet cakes

This route works best when the sweet potatoes can be pressed together without turning completely smooth. Mash them lightly with a fork, leaving a few small pieces for texture. Add a handful of crushed beans, chopped scallions, breadcrumbs or oats, and a pinch of smoked paprika or cumin.

Shape the mixture into squat patties and cook them in a lightly oiled skillet over medium heat until both sides are deeply golden. Serve with a sharp yogurt-and-lemon sauce, a spoonful of salsa, or a pile of dressed greens.

Good supporting ingredients: black beans, white beans, feta, scallions, parsley, eggs, breadcrumbs, hot sauce.

Choose this when: you want a crisp-edged dinner and have ten minutes to stand near the stove.

2. Turn them into smoky bean tacos

Warm the sweet potatoes in a skillet with black beans or pinto beans, cumin, smoked paprika, and a splash of water or tomato sauce to bring everything together. Keep some cubes intact so the filling has little sweet, browned bites among the beans.

Spoon into warm tortillas and add something bright: lime, chopped onion, cabbage, herbs, or a quick salsa. A dollop of yogurt, sour cream, or avocado adds a cool counterpoint.

Good supporting ingredients: tortillas, canned beans, tomato paste, salsa, lime, cabbage, grated cheese, avocado.

Choose this when: the table needs a build-your-own dinner and the cupboard can do most of the work.

3. Give a quick soup a sweet-potato finish

A modest amount of roasted sweet potato can make a brothy soup feel complete. Start with onion, garlic, ginger, or curry powder in a pot. Add stock or water, a can of beans or lentils, and your sweet potatoes. Blend part of the pot for body, then leave the rest chunky.

Coconut milk is optional; a spoonful can round out the texture. Finish with lemon, herbs, chilli crisp, or toasted seeds. The aim is a relaxed bowl with enough texture to keep every spoonful interesting.

Good supporting ingredients: onion, garlic, ginger, canned beans, coconut milk, curry powder, lemon, spinach, herbs.

Choose this when: your sweet potatoes are soft and you want dinner to happen mostly in one pot.

4. Build a baked tray with eggs or beans

Scatter sweet potato pieces in a baking dish with greens, beans, or halved tomatoes. Add eggs for a soft-set centre, or cover the tray with cheese and crunchy breadcrumbs for a more substantial finish. A drizzle of olive oil and a pinch of chilli flakes are usually enough seasoning when the potatoes are already well roasted.

Bake until the supporting ingredients are hot and the top has a little colour. Bring the dish straight to the table with toast, flatbread, or a simple salad.

Good supporting ingredients: eggs, chickpeas, kale, spinach, tomatoes, feta, cheddar, breadcrumbs, flatbread.

Choose this when: you want the oven to take over while you clear the counter or set the table.

A simple way to add more flavour

Roasted sweet potatoes pair especially well with ingredients that bring salt, sharpness, heat, or crunch. Pick one from each column and dinner will feel more deliberate.

Add salt Add sharpness Add heat Add crunch
feta, grated cheese, olives lemon, lime, vinegar, yogurt chilli flakes, hot sauce, smoked paprika toasted seeds, nuts, breadcrumbs, crisp cabbage

You only need a few supporting ingredients. Let the cooked sweet potatoes stay visible in the final dish; their browned edges and soft texture are the reason each route works.
