What to Make With Ricotta: 5 Savory Dinner Routes for a Tub in the Fridge

A tub of ricotta can carry a whole dinner. Its mild flavour works with sharp greens, tomatoes, lemon, beans, pasta, potatoes, and crisp breadcrumbs. Start by deciding what you want from tonight: a soft baked centre, a quick skillet meal, something filled, a creamy sauce, or a crisp-edged fritter.

Use this guide as a route selector. Each route gives ricotta a different job, so you can choose from the ingredients and equipment already within reach.

A small tub suits sauce, skillet, or fritter routes. Use more generous spoonfuls for baking and stuffing.

1. Build a baked ricotta tray

Choose this route when you have an oven, a vegetable that can roast, and enough time to let the edges turn golden.

Start with:

How to assemble it

Serve it with bread, a grain, or a salad. Leftover beans turn it into a more substantial tray with very little extra work.

  • sliced aubergine, courgette, mushrooms, peppers, tomatoes, or cooked greens;
  • ricotta;
  • something sharp: lemon zest, grated hard cheese, capers, chilli, or herbs;
  • breadcrumbs or torn bread for the top.
  1. Roast or sauté the vegetables until they have taken on some colour.
  2. Stir ricotta with lemon zest, salt, pepper, herbs, and a little grated cheese if you have it.
  3. Layer the vegetables and ricotta in a shallow dish.
  4. Cover with breadcrumbs, oil, and a little more cheese.
  5. Bake until hot through and crisp on top.

2. Finish a quick skillet with ricotta

Choose this route for a weeknight pan of vegetables, beans, greens, or gnocchi. Ricotta goes in at the end, where it adds soft pockets and takes the edge off a spicy or tomato-rich base.

Good starting points:

Cook the base first. Add a little water, stock, or pasta water if the pan needs loosening. Spoon ricotta over the top, cover briefly, then finish with lemon, black pepper, herbs, or a drizzle of olive oil.

Keep the ricotta in visible spoonfuls. Each serving gets a creamy point of contrast while the pan stays light.

  • white beans with kale or spinach;
  • cherry tomatoes with chickpeas;
  • mushrooms with peas;
  • cooked gnocchi with greens;
  • leftover roasted vegetables warmed with garlic and chilli.

3. Use ricotta as a seasoned filling

Choose this route when you have something to fill: large pasta shells, cannelloni, tortillas, pitta, baked potatoes, mushrooms, or halved peppers.

Mix ricotta with one strong flavour direction:

Direction Add to the ricotta Pair with
Green and lemony Lemon zest, herbs, wilted greens Pasta shells, potatoes, mushrooms
Tomato and chilli Tomato paste, chilli flakes, herbs Tortillas, peppers, baked pasta
Bean and herb Mashed beans, parsley, garlic Pitta, wraps, courgettes
Smoky and earthy Paprika, roasted vegetables, pepper Sweet potatoes, peppers, pasta bake

Fill the chosen base, add a small amount of sauce or vegetables around it, and bake or pan-warm until the centre is hot. The filling works best when one element is bright or savoury enough to lead the flavour.

4. Turn ricotta into a quick dinner sauce

Ricotta makes a fast sauce for pasta, gnocchi, beans, or roasted vegetables. It needs a little warm liquid and a bright finishing ingredient to become smooth and lively.

For two portions, combine in a bowl:

Toss with hot pasta or gnocchi until the sauce coats everything. Fold in peas, spinach, roasted vegetables, beans, or shredded chicken if you have them.

For a tomato version, warm tomato sauce first, then add the ricotta in spoonfuls at the end. The separate creamy pockets make a simple sauce feel more generous.

  • 4 to 6 spoonfuls of ricotta;
  • a splash of pasta water, stock, or hot water;
  • lemon zest or a squeeze of lemon;
  • black pepper;
  • grated cheese, herbs, chilli, or chopped olives if available.

5. Make crisp ricotta fritters

This route is useful when you have a small amount of ricotta plus cooked potato, courgette, greens, grains, or vegetables that need a new shape.

Mix ricotta with the cooked ingredient, an egg if you use eggs, a little flour or breadcrumbs, and a strong seasoning such as herbs, lemon, chilli, garlic, or grated cheese. Form small patties and cook in a lightly oiled pan until both sides are browned.

Serve fritters with:

Aim for a mixture that holds together when pressed. If it feels loose, add breadcrumbs a spoonful at a time.

  • a tomato salad;
  • fried or poached eggs;
  • beans dressed with lemon;
  • a spoonful of yoghurt or a sharp green sauce;
  • bread and a handful of leaves.
