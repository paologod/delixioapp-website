How to Use Half a Jar of Pesto: 5 Dinner Routes Beyond Pasta

A half jar of pesto can carry dinner much further than a bowl of pasta. Treat it as a concentrated flavor starter: add a sturdy base, something fresh or warm, and a little texture. The best route depends on the ingredients already around it.

Quick answer: choose your pesto route

If you have… Make… Why it works
Bread, cheese, tomatoes, or vegetables A pesto flatbread Fast, crisp, and easy to share
Tinned beans and greens A white-bean pesto skillet A filling one-pan dinner with a creamy finish
Eggs and a few vegetables Pesto eggs or a frittata A flexible use for small amounts of pesto
Cooked grains or potatoes A warm pesto bowl Great for mixed fridge ingredients
Roasted or sautéed vegetables A pesto traybake finish A quick way to pull separate ingredients together

Start with the route that matches what you have. Pesto is usually strongest when stirred through, spooned on at the end, or used as a finishing layer rather than cooked hard for a long time.

Route 1: Make a quick pesto flatbread

Best for: bread, wraps, naan, pitta, or an open packet of tortillas.

A flatbread works especially well when the jar is nearly empty: use a spoonful of pesto, then loosen the last streaks in the jar with a little olive oil or lemon juice and drizzle it over the top.

  1. Spread pesto over the base, leaving a small border.
  2. Add a thin layer of cheese if you have it.
  3. Top with tomatoes, mushrooms, roasted vegetables, onions, or leftover chicken.
  4. Bake or toast until the edges are crisp and the topping is hot.
  5. Finish with salad leaves, pepper, lemon, or a few nuts if they are around.

Route 2: Turn pesto and beans into a skillet dinner

Best for: cannellini beans, butter beans, chickpeas, lentils, spinach, kale, peas, or any quick-cooking greens.

Warm olive oil in a pan with garlic or onion if available. Add drained beans and any vegetables that need cooking. Stir in a splash of water, stock, or the liquid from a tin of tomatoes to make the pan glossy. Take the pan off the heat, then fold through pesto a spoonful at a time.

Serve with toast, rice, couscous, or potatoes. Grated cheese, a fried egg, or a few torn pieces of mozzarella can make it more substantial.

A useful proportion

For two portions, begin with one tin of beans and 2 to 3 tablespoons of pesto. Add more pesto after tasting; different jars vary a lot in saltiness and intensity.

Route 3: Give eggs a pesto shortcut

Best for: a small amount of pesto plus eggs, cheese, or any cooked vegetables.

For fast eggs, warm a teaspoon of pesto in a nonstick pan, crack in two eggs, then cook gently until the whites are set. Add a little water and cover the pan briefly if you like a soft yolk.

For a frittata, whisk eggs with a spoonful of pesto and a handful of cooked vegetables. Cook slowly in an oven-safe pan, then finish under the grill or in the oven. Serve with bread and a sharp salad.

This route is handy when there is enough pesto to flavor a dish but not enough to make a sauce.

Route 4: Build a warm pesto bowl from cooked bits

Best for: leftover rice, couscous, quinoa, potatoes, roasted vegetables, beans, greens, or a small piece of protein.

Use this simple bowl formula:

Warm the base and filling first. Spoon the loosened pesto over at the end, then add the fresh and crunchy elements. It gives the bowl definition without requiring a separate dressing.

  • Base: cooked grain or potatoes
  • Filling: beans, egg, chicken, tofu, or cheese
  • Vegetable: anything roasted, sautéed, steamed, or raw and crisp
  • Pesto finish: loosen pesto with a spoonful of warm water, olive oil, or lemon juice
  • Texture: seeds, nuts, breadcrumbs, crisp onions, or torn toast

Route 5: Use pesto as the final layer on a traybake

Best for: roasted vegetables, potatoes, sausages, chicken thighs, chickpeas, or halloumi.

Roast your main ingredients with oil, salt, and pepper until cooked through and browned. Let the tray sit for a minute, then dot pesto over the warm ingredients and toss gently. Add lemon, yoghurt, or a handful of leaves if you have them.

The pesto stays bright and fresh when it meets the tray near the end. It also helps a simple roast become a meal that feels intentional.

Make the jar useful again tomorrow

If your pesto dinner uses only part of the jar, plan the next use while the ideas are still visible: a sandwich spread, a dressing for a grain salad, a spoonful in scrambled eggs, or a finish for soup. Small, planned repeat uses can make one jar feel like a flexible kitchen ingredient rather than a one-recipe purchase.
