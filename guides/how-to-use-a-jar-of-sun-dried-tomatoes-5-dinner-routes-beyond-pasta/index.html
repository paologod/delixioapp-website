How to Use a Jar of Sun-Dried Tomatoes: 5 Dinner Routes Beyond Pasta

An open jar of sun-dried tomatoes is more useful than a single pasta night. Think of them as a concentrated savoury accent: a small amount can add sweetness, acidity, and depth to ingredients that otherwise need help.

The easiest way to use them is to choose a dinner shape first. Then decide whether the tomatoes should be blended, chopped, softened, or scattered at the end.

Start here: choose the route that matches tonight

If your jar is packed in oil, use a little of that oil for the pan or dressing. It already carries the tomato flavour. If the tomatoes are dry-packed, cover them with hot water for a few minutes before chopping; they will be easier to blend and gentler in the finished dish.

Route 1: Make a sun-dried tomato bean skillet

Choose this when: you want a warm, one-pan dinner with beans as the centre.

The tomatoes belong early in this route. Softening them in the pan lets their flavour move through the beans rather than sitting in separate chewy pieces.

Good pairings: cannellini beans with spinach and lemon; chickpeas with cumin and yogurt; black beans with corn and lime.

  1. Warm a little oil in a skillet.
  2. Add garlic, onion, or chilli flakes if you have them.
  3. Add chopped sun-dried tomatoes and let them soften for a minute.
  4. Stir in drained beans and a splash of water, stock, or tomato liquid.
  5. Finish with greens, lemon, cheese, or an egg if available.

Route 2: Fill tortillas, pitas, or toast with tomato-and-egg folds

Choose this when: you have eggs and need dinner quickly.

Finely chop two or three sun-dried tomatoes. Add them to scrambled eggs, a folded omelette, or a warm bean-and-egg tortilla with a handful of cheese or greens.

Here, the tomatoes should be small and evenly distributed. Their job is to season the filling, not to dominate every bite.

Route 3: Turn grains into a complete bowl

Choose this when: you have cooked rice, couscous, quinoa, bulgur, or another grain to use.

Stir chopped sun-dried tomatoes into warm grains with one rich element and one fresh element. The tomatoes are best used near the end, so they stay distinct and add little bursts of flavour.

Try this formula:

Base Protein or substance Sun-dried tomato partner Fresh finish
Couscous Chickpeas Feta or olives Lemon and parsley
Rice Leftover chicken Spinach or roasted peppers Yogurt or herbs
Quinoa White beans Artichokes or cucumber Olive oil and vinegar

The point is not to reproduce a strict recipe. It is to make one open jar useful alongside the ingredients already in your kitchen.

Route 4: Blend a quick sauce for vegetables, toast, or a tray dinner

Choose this when: you have vegetables that need a clearer flavour direction.

Blend softened sun-dried tomatoes with olive oil, a splash of water, lemon juice or vinegar, and one optional creamy ingredient such as yogurt, tahini, or a spoonful of beans. The result should be loose enough to spoon, not a thick paste.

Use it to:

This is the route where sun-dried tomatoes work as a sauce base, not a topping. Blending avoids tough pieces and spreads the flavour further.

  • dress roasted vegetables after they come out of the oven
  • spread onto toast before adding beans, eggs, or greens
  • spoon over a warm potato or grain bowl
  • coat vegetables before a short roast

Route 5: Build a fast soup or braise

Choose this when: you have lentils, beans, broth, leftover chicken, or vegetables that can simmer for a few minutes.

Add chopped sun-dried tomatoes to the pan with your aromatics, then add your main ingredient and enough liquid to make it saucy or brothy. Simmer until the tomatoes are tender. Blend part of the liquid if you want a thicker texture.

Useful combinations include:

A small amount goes a long way. Start with two or three pieces, taste, then add more. Their saltiness and concentration can build quickly.

  • red lentils, carrots, sun-dried tomatoes, and broth
  • white beans, kale, sun-dried tomatoes, and lemon
  • leftover chicken, peas, sun-dried tomatoes, and stock

A simple pairing map

Very salty Unsalted beans, grains, potatoes, or a splash of water
Very rich Lemon, vinegar, cucumber, herbs, or greens
Too chewy Hot water, stock, or a few minutes in the pan
A little flat Garlic, chilli, olive oil, or a creamy element

1. Use a little, then taste

Sun-dried tomatoes are concentrated. You often need fewer than you think, especially when they are oil-packed.

2. Match the cut to the job

Blend them for a sauce, chop them for a bean skillet or grain bowl, and mince them for eggs or fillings.

3. Give them a counterpoint

They shine next to something mild, creamy, fresh, or starchy. Beans, eggs, grains, potatoes, greens, yogurt, and lemon all make them feel more like dinner than garnish.

Can I use dry-packed sun-dried tomatoes?

Yes. Soften them in hot water for a few minutes before chopping or blending. Save a little soaking liquid for soup, beans, or sauce if it tastes good.

What if I do not have pasta?

That is exactly where these routes help. Use the jar with beans, eggs, grains, potatoes, vegetables, toast, or a quick soup instead.

How long does an opened jar keep?

Follow the storage guidance on its label. Keep the tomatoes covered as directed, use clean utensils, and discard them if you notice an off smell, mould, or other signs of spoilage.

The takeaway

Sun-dried tomatoes are not just for pasta. Use them early for a bean skillet or braise, finely chopped for eggs, stirred through grains at the end, or blended when your vegetables need a fast sauce. Pick the dinner shape first, then let the jar do one clear job.
