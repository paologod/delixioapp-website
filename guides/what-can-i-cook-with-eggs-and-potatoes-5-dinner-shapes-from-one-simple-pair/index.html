What Can I Cook With Eggs and Potatoes? 5 Dinner Shapes From One Simple Pair

Eggs and potatoes can become a very good dinner without a long shopping list. The useful question is which dinner shape fits the time, pan, and extras you have tonight.

Quick answer

Start with potatoes as the filling base, then choose one of these five directions:

If you want… Make… Best extra to add
Crisp edges and a fast skillet Potato-and-egg hash Onion or greens
A sliceable one-pan dinner Tortilla-style potato skillet Onion
A softer, low-effort bake Potato frittata Cheese or vegetables
Something that feels more assembled Loaded baked potatoes with eggs Cheese, beans, or salsa
A way to use already-cooked potatoes Potato cakes with a fried egg Herbs or cheese

You only need eggs, potatoes, cooking oil or butter, salt, and pepper to begin. Everything else is a useful branch, not a requirement.

First, pick the potato route

Raw potatoes and 20-30 minutes: slice or dice them small so they cook evenly in a skillet. A lid helps them soften before you uncover the pan for colour.

Raw potatoes and an oven: bake or roast them until tender, then finish with eggs for a more hands-off dinner.

Cooked potatoes from another meal: use them for potato cakes or a quick hash. Their softer texture makes the fast routes especially easy.

The best route is the one that matches your evening. If you are stuck between several ingredients and no clear plan, Delixio’s guide to deciding what to cook when the fridge looks empty uses the same practical idea: choose a dinner shape before worrying about a perfect recipe.

1. Crisp potato-and-egg hash

Choose this when: you want a skillet dinner with browned bits and you can spare about 25 minutes.

If you also have onions: cook them with the potatoes from the start.

If you also have greens: stir them in just before the eggs go in.

If you also have canned beans: add them once the potatoes are nearly tender; they make the skillet more substantial without changing the method.

  1. Dice potatoes into small pieces. Cook them in a wide pan with oil, salt, and pepper over medium heat, stirring occasionally.
  2. When the potatoes are tender and starting to crisp, make two or three small gaps in the pan.
  3. Crack an egg into each gap, cover the pan, and cook until the whites are set to your liking.
  4. Finish with anything bright or salty you have: chopped herbs, cheese, hot sauce, a spoonful of yoghurt, or a squeeze of lemon.

2. Tortilla-style potato skillet

Choose this when: you want a soft-centred, sliceable dinner and have a little more patience.

Thinly slice potatoes and, if you have one, an onion. Cook them gently in enough oil to keep them from catching, until the potatoes are tender. Beat eggs with seasoning, fold in the potato mixture, then cook in a non-stick or well-seasoned pan over low heat until mostly set. Finish under a grill if that is easy in your kitchen, or cover the pan and let residual heat do the work.

This is a flexible home version rather than a strict technique test. Add parsley, chives, a little cheese, or cooked greens if they are around. Serve it with a simple salad, tomatoes, or a spoonful of something sharp from the fridge.

3. Potato frittata

Choose this when: you have leftover roasted or boiled potatoes, a few vegetables, or an oven-safe pan.

A frittata is the calm option: it forgives small amounts of leftover ingredients and does not need much stirring.

Cut it into wedges and call it dinner. Any extra slices can become tomorrow’s lunch, which is why this format is especially useful when you have a few disconnected bits to use.

  • Scatter bite-size cooked potatoes in an oven-safe skillet or baking dish.
  • Add any cooked vegetables you want to use: mushrooms, peppers, spinach, courgette, or onions all work.
  • Pour over beaten eggs seasoned with salt and pepper.
  • Add cheese if you have it, then bake until the centre is just set.

4. Loaded potatoes with eggs

Choose this when: you want dinner to feel more like separate components on a plate.

Bake or microwave potatoes until tender, split them open, then add an egg on top. A fried egg gives the fastest result; a poached or softly scrambled egg works too. Finish with the toppings your kitchen can support:

This route is handy when people at the table want different toppings. Put the potatoes and eggs at the centre, then let everyone build their own version.

  • cheese and black beans;
  • sautéed greens and garlic;
  • yoghurt and herbs;
  • leftover chilli or lentils;
  • tomatoes, salsa, or a spoonful of pesto.

5. Potato cakes with a fried egg

Choose this when: you have cooked potatoes and want the quickest transformation.

Mash cooked potatoes roughly with salt, pepper, and a small amount of flour or breadcrumbs if the mixture needs help holding together. Cheese, spring onions, herbs, or leftover vegetables can go in too. Shape into small patties and fry until crisp on both sides. Top with a fried egg.

The contrast of a crisp potato cake and a soft egg makes a small amount of leftovers feel deliberate. Serve with a green salad, roasted vegetables, or whatever fresh element is easiest to reach for.

A simple chooser for tonight

  • Only one pan and limited time? Make the hash.
  • Want slices for the table or lunch tomorrow? Make the tortilla-style skillet or frittata.
  • Have cooked potatoes already? Make potato cakes.
  • Want everyone to customise their own plate? Make loaded potatoes.
  • Have vegetables that would be useful in dinner? Fold them into a frittata or hash.

Common mistakes that make this pair feel flat

Keeping potato pieces too large. Small dice or thin slices help the potatoes cook before the outside gets too dark.

Adding eggs before the potatoes are ready. Get the potato texture where you want it first; eggs cook quickly.

Using the same seasoning every time. Potatoes and eggs welcome contrast. Try herbs, chilli, mustard, a sharp cheese, lemon, or a spoonful of yoghurt depending on what is already open.

Treating missing extras as a reason to abandon the idea. Eggs and potatoes are the core. One useful extra is plenty.

FAQ

Can I make dinner with only eggs and potatoes?

Yes. Cook the potatoes in a skillet until tender, season well, and add eggs near the end. Onion, cheese, herbs, beans, or greens add variety, but they are optional.

What is the fastest egg and potato dinner?

A hash made with small diced potatoes is usually the fastest route from raw potatoes. Potato cakes are faster when the potatoes are already cooked.

What can I add to eggs and potatoes?

Onion, cheese, greens, canned beans, herbs, yoghurt, salsa, and leftover cooked vegetables all work. Choose one or two extras rather than trying to add everything at once.
