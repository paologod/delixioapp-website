What Can I Use Instead of Tomato Paste? A Pantry Substitution Guide for Weeknight Cooking

A recipe calls for one spoonful of tomato paste, and the tube is empty. Dinner can still move forward.

Tomato paste usually brings concentrated tomato flavour, a little acidity, savoury depth, colour, and body. The best substitute depends on which of those jobs matters most in your dish. A thin soup needs a different adjustment from a thick skillet sauce.

Quick answer

For 1 tablespoon tomato paste, use one of these starting points:

If you have… Start with… Best in Small adjustment
Canned crushed or puréed tomatoes 3 tablespoons soups, sauces, braises Cook a little longer to reduce extra liquid.
Tomato sauce or passata 3 tablespoons pasta sauce, soups Simmer until it tastes more concentrated.
Canned diced tomatoes 4 tablespoons, blended or finely chopped soups, chilli-style skillets Reduce briefly before adding more liquid.
Roasted red peppers from a jar 2 tablespoons, blended creamy sauces, stews, skillet dinners Add a small splash of vinegar or lemon for brightness if the dish needs it.
Ketchup 1 tablespoon quick sauces, glazes Use sparingly; it is sweeter than tomato paste.
Sun-dried tomatoes 1 tablespoon, finely chopped or blended pasta, grain dishes, sauces Add water or oil as needed to help it disperse.

These are cooking adjustments, not exact replacements. Taste as the dish cooks and decide whether it needs more tomato character, more salt, more acidity, or a longer simmer.

Build a deep base for a soup, stew, or braise

Choose crushed tomatoes, passata, or tomato sauce. Add about three times the amount of paste called for, then give the dish a few extra minutes so the tomato flavour can concentrate.

If the recipe begins by cooking tomato paste with onions, garlic, or spices, add your substitute after those ingredients have softened. Let it bubble gently rather than rushing straight to broth.

Thicken and colour a skillet sauce

Choose passata, crushed tomatoes, or blended roasted red peppers. They add colour and character, although they are looser than paste. Keep the pan uncovered for a few minutes and stir occasionally. The sauce will tell you when it has found its shape.

Add a small tomato note to a fast meal

Choose ketchup or sun-dried tomatoes when the recipe needs only a spoonful. Ketchup works best in a quick glaze, a burger-style skillet, or a casual sauce where a hint of sweetness fits. Sun-dried tomatoes bring a punchier, savoury result for pasta, beans, or couscous.

Make a dish feel rounder without a strong tomato taste

Choose roasted red peppers. Blend or mash them before adding. They bring colour and sweetness with a gentler tomato profile, which can suit creamy sauces or a vegetable-heavy pan.

A simple decision tree

Do you have a tomato product?

  • Yes: crushed tomatoes, passata, sauce, or diced tomatoes → use it, then reduce the dish a little longer.
  • No, but you have sun-dried tomatoes → chop or blend them with a splash of water; use a smaller amount because their flavour is concentrated.
  • No, but you have jarred roasted red peppers → blend them for a mellow, colourful substitute.
  • Only ketchup? → use it for a small, quick substitute where a touch of sweetness will not feel out of place.
  • None of these? → leave out the tomato element and build flavour from the ingredients that are already in the pan. A meal can still be satisfying when its direction changes slightly.

How to adjust the rest of the recipe

A substitute is easier to manage when you make one adjustment at a time.

If the dish looks too loose

Leave the lid off and simmer gently. Extra tomato liquid needs time to cook down. For a skillet dinner, move ingredients aside briefly so the sauce has contact with the hot pan.

If it tastes flat

Try a small pinch of salt, a little grated cheese if it suits the recipe, or a savoury pantry ingredient such as soy sauce. Add gradually; these ingredients can change a dish quickly.

If it tastes too sweet

Balance with a small squeeze of lemon, a splash of vinegar, or a pinch of chilli. This is especially useful when ketchup or roasted peppers stand in for tomato paste.

If it needs more body

Keep simmering first. If the recipe allows it, a spoonful of mashed beans, blended vegetables, or a little starchy pasta water can help a sauce cling together without sending you to the shop.

Pasta sauce

Use passata or crushed tomatoes. Start with three tablespoons for every tablespoon of tomato paste, cook it down with garlic and olive oil, then toss with pasta. Sun-dried tomatoes can add extra depth if you have them.

Bean skillet

Use diced tomatoes, passata, or roasted red peppers. Let the liquid reduce before adding beans so the final sauce stays spoonable rather than soupy.

Soup or lentil pot

Use crushed tomatoes or tomato sauce. The broth can absorb the extra liquid, so the main change is a slightly longer simmer.

Quick glaze for roasted vegetables or tofu

Use ketchup in a small quantity, then balance it with something savoury and a little acid. This is a different flavour direction from tomato paste, but it can work beautifully for a fast, sticky finish.

Pantry habit: give concentrated ingredients a backup role

Tomato paste is useful because a small amount changes a whole pan. Keeping one backup option in mind makes weeknight substitutions much calmer:

  • canned tomatoes for flexible sauces and soups;
  • sun-dried tomatoes for concentrated savoury flavour;
  • roasted red peppers for mellow depth and colour;
  • ketchup for the occasional fast glaze.

When the missing ingredient is only the beginning

One absent ingredient rarely has to end the plan. Look at what the dish still has: a base, something filling, a vegetable, and a flavour direction. Then choose the substitute that helps those pieces work together.

FAQ

Can I use ketchup instead of tomato paste?

Yes, for a small amount in a quick sauce or glaze. Ketchup is sweeter and more seasoned, so it is less suited to dishes where tomato paste is the main savoury base.

Can I use tomato sauce instead of tomato paste?

Yes. Start with about three times as much, then simmer the dish a little longer to concentrate it.

Do I need to replace tomato paste at all?

No. If the recipe uses only a small amount, you can often continue and adjust the seasoning at the end. The dish may take a slightly different direction, which is part of everyday cooking.
